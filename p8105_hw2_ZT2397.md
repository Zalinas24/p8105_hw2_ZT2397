``` r
  pull(weight_tons) |>
  sum()

gwynnda_june_2022_butts =
  trash_df |>

  pull(cigarette_butts) |>

```

Putting the three trash wheels together gave me one dataset with 1269
observations (740 dumpsters from mr. Trash Wheel, 137 from Professor
Trash Wheel, and 392 from Gwynnda) and 15 variables. Each row is one
emptied dumpster. To keep track of which wheel collected what, I added a
variable for the trash wheel name. The data set also records the
dumpster number, month, year, and date, followed by the weight and the
volume. The rest of the variables count the items collected including
plastic bottles, polystyrene, cigarette butts, glass bottles, plastic
bags, wrappers, and sports balls. The last variable is the number of
homes powered. Not every wheel tracks every item. Sports balls were only
counted for Mr. Trash Wheel, and Gwynnda has no glass bottle counts, so
those values are missing for the others. Across all of its dumpsters,
Professor Trash Wheel collected 294.96 tons of trash, and Gwynnda
collected 18,120 cigarette butts in June 2022.

Both files have a line of description above the real column names, so I
skipped it when importing. In the baseline data, a missing age at onset
is written as a period, which means the participant stayed free of MCI
during follow-up. Sex and APOE4 status were stored as 0 and 1, so I
recoded them as male / female and carrier / non-carrier. To meet the
study’s inclusion criteria, I removed participants who already had MCI
at baseline, meaning their age at onset was at or before their baseline
age. 483 participants were recruited, and 4 of them already had MCI at
baseline, leaving 479. Of these, 93 developed MCI during follow-up. The
average baseline age is 65.03 years, and the proportion of women in the
study who are APOE4 carriers is 0.3.

The biomarker file also has a description line to skip, and it uses “Na”
as well as “NA” for missing values, so both are treated as missing.
Without that, the amyloid columns would import as text. I renamed the
study ID variable to `id` so it matches the baseline data, then pivoted
the visits into long format so each row is one participant at one visit.
The time variable is years since baseline (0, 2, 4, 6, and 8). The
dataset has 487 participants and 2435 rows, and some visits are missing
an amyloid ratio.
